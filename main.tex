\section{SNR of raw data}\label{sec:snr}
% =====================================================================
% Manual 2.3.1 For an explanation of SNR, you can refer to the Canvas page for Learning Task
% on SNR estimation.

% Manual 2.3.1a Calculate the SNR for all four signals and tabulate them. Show using a figure
% how you calculated SNR. Comment on the important points to pay attention in calculating SNR.
\paragraph{(a)} The SNR is estimated in the frequency domain, following the Learning Task on SNR
estimation. For each signal the mean is removed and the power per frequency bin is calculated
over $t = 35$--45~s ($N = 10240$, $\Delta f = 0.1$~Hz),
\[
  P[k] = \frac{2\,|X[k]|^2}{N^2},
\]
so that $\sum_k P[k]$ is the mean power of the signal (Parseval). The signal is the heartbeat,
which lies in the band $B_s$ = 0.5--40~Hz for the ECG and 0.5--10~Hz for sig-P. Noise is also
present inside $B_s$. Its level $\rho$ (power per Hz) is measured in a signal-free band $B_n$ just
above $B_s$ (60--90~Hz for the ECG, 15--35~Hz for sig-P) and extrapolated into $B_s$
(Figure~\ref{fig:snr}). Everything that is not signal counts as noise:
\[
  P_s = \sum_{k \in B_s} P[k] - \rho\,|B_s|, \qquad
  P_n = \sum_k P[k] - P_s, \qquad
  \mathrm{SNR} = 10\log_{10}\frac{P_s}{P_n}.
\]
Table~\ref{tab:snr} gives the results. sig-F has the highest SNR of the three ECG signals and is
used in the rest of this report.

Points to pay attention to when calculating the SNR:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The SNR depends on how signal and noise are separated. The band edges must be chosen
        from the spectrum and kept the same for every comparison.
  \item The mean (DC offset) must be removed, otherwise it counts as signal power.
  \item The noise band must be free of signal, and the extrapolation assumes the noise is flat
        over $B_s$. The ECG noise floor rises towards low frequencies, so the noise inside $B_s$
        is underestimated.
  \item Baseline drift below 0.5~Hz is 60--85\% of the ECG noise power, so the SNR depends
        strongly on the time window. The same window must be used for all signals.
  \item The SNR is a power ratio, so it is converted to dB with $10\log_{10}$, not $20\log_{10}$.
\end{itemize}
